PAD — inverse-dynamics encoder adaptation

Policy Adaptation during Deployment: update the world-model encoder online with a self-supervised inverse-dynamics loss that predicts the executed action from consecutive latents. The inverse-dynamics head is pretrained offline on the same PushObj trajectory pool used to train the released world models, then reset alongside the encoder every episode.

Reference: Hansen et al., 'Self-Supervised Policy Adaptation during Deployment', ICLR 2021

Overview

PAD (Hansen et al.) adapts the world-model encoder during deployment using an inverse-dynamics objective: from the latent before and after an executed action chunk, an auxiliary head predicts that chunk. This differs materially from the existing arms: the head is a module owned by the adapter, pretrained offline and then optimized with the encoder online.

The head is pretrained on 2,048 transition pairs from the existing PushObj training set, using frozen base latents. train_inverse_head.py records the checkpoint and is the source for checkpoints/pad/pushobj_inverse_dynamics.pth. At deployment PAD updates both the encoder and head on the most recently executed model transition; it declares episode isolation because the optimizer owns shared weights.

The original PAD head is jointly pretrained with the policy. This benchmark has a world model and MPC planner rather than that policy, so the shipped head is the closest honest substitute: it is pretrained on the available offline transitions but never on selection or test episodes. Results should be read as a test of whether this auxiliary signal transfers to latent world-model control, not a bit-identical reproduction of the original policy architecture.
